# 29: Tune Thresholds To Reduce Noise

**Specification Version:** 1.0.0

**Status:** Accepted

**Slug:** 29-tune-thresholds

**Implementation Types:**

* None

## Descriptive Statement

Tune thresholds regularly to reduce noise, increase trust.

## Supplemental Guidance

CRAWL (0-14) Thresholds are whatever the tool shipped with. Nobody counts false positives, nobody knows
what was missed, and alerts are muted rather than tuned.

WALK (15-30) Precision is measured and thresholds are reviewed on a schedule. Changes are written down
with a reason, but they are still made by one person without the recipients in the room.

RUN (31-42) Precision and missed anomalies are both tracked. Tuning happens monthly or better, changes
are reviewed with the people who receive the alerts, and threshold state is version controlled.

FLY (43-50) Tuning is continuous or automated. A proposed threshold is replayed against historical
anomalies before release, so the team knows what a change would have missed before it ships.

Precision is weighted highest because noise is what destroys trust in the alert, and a channel that
owners have learned to ignore scores zero on every other action in this capability.

Missed anomalies is weighted second because a quiet channel is not the same as a healthy one. A team
that raises thresholds until precision looks perfect will show it here.

## Scoring Guide

**Weight:** 1.0

### Formula

* Weighted sum
  Alert precision (score x4),
  Missed anomalies (score x3),
  Tuning cadence (score x2),
  Tuning governance (score x1).
  Max raw (50)

* Alert_Precision: Alerts confirmed as true positives divided by all alerts raised in the review period
  (0=Not measured, 1<50%, 2=50-69%, 3=70-84%, 4=85-94%, 5=95%+)
* Missed_Anomalies: Share of anomalies first found by invoice review, budget variance, or an owner
  (0=Not measured, 1>25%, 2=10-25%, 3=5-9%, 4=1-4%, 5<1%)
* Tuning_Cadence: How often thresholds are reviewed and adjusted against observed outcomes
  (0=Never, 1=Ad-hoc, 2=Annual, 3=Quarterly, 4=Monthly, 5=Continuous or automated)
* Tuning_Governance: How a threshold change is decided and recorded
  (0=Undocumented, 1=Changed by one person, 2=Documented with rationale,
  3=Reviewed with the alert recipients, 4=Version controlled and auditable,
  5=Version controlled and replayed against historical anomalies before release)

* Output Range (0-50)

### Scoring Details

* [Nist Csf 2.0 - De.ae-08](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.29): "Incidents are declared when adverse events meet the defined incident criteria." A threshold is that
criteria, which is why tuning it is a governed change and not a personal preference.
* [Nist Csf 2.0 - Id.im-01](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.29): "Improvements are identified from evaluations." Precision and missed anomalies are the evaluation that
tells a team whether the last tuning change helped.
* [Finops Kpi - Anomaly Detection Rate](https://www.finops.org/kpi/anomaly-detection-rate/): A useful companion signal. A detection rate that drops after a tuning change is either a real
improvement or a rise in missed anomalies, and only the second measure here separates the two.
