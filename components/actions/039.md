## Descriptive Statement

Run rolling forecasts and determine refresh frequency

## Supplemental Guidance

A few definitions are impportant to understand before scoring this action. First, there is the Time Horizon
which defines the point in time you are forecasting out to. For example, this could be next month's spend or
total spend at the end of the year. A Rolling Forecast would mean that your time horizon is always a set time
out from the date of the forecast. For example, you could forecast the next three months and each month you
create a new forecast for the following three months, and so on.

Next, we have the forecast Frequency. This is how often you update your forecast based on the speed of change
in your hosting environments. Are big changes happening rapidly, or is there a slow stable trend? This will
determine if you need to update the forecast more rapidly based on certain driver variables.
This is the definition we are using to determine scores in this action, as higher frequency is more accurate.

There is also the Backwards Horizon where you set a time limit on how much historical data to bring in. Do we
only care about the last three months of data? Or do we need to look at previous years to determine a
seasonality pattern? Has there been a significant optimization or change that makes data from last month no
longer representative of the usage of a particular team? If so, make sure to only pull in relevant data.

Lastly, we have forecast Granularity. This is the sum of the cost over a set time period. For example, are
you predicting costs for a year to plan EDP contracts? Do you need to provide a quarterly number for updates
to Wall Street? Or do you need Daily numbers to determine capacity planning? This depends on the decisions you
need to make as a result of doing this forecast.
