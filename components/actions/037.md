# 37: Establish Forecasting Approach

**Specification Version:** 2.0.0

**Status:** Accepted

**Slug:** 37-forecast-approach

**Implementation Types:**

* None

## Descriptive Statement

Establish "why," approach (static/trend/driver-based), cadence, and horizon.

## Supplemental Guidance

The three techniques in item 2 come from the FinOps Framework. A static forecast predicts for the fiscal
year with no adjustments. A trend-based forecast uses historic trends to project future spend, ideally
taking seasonality into account, but it will not capture out-of-band events such as launching a new
product. A driver-based forecast uses a business KPI to project spend, applying the KPI growth to actual
cost, but it will not forecast workloads that do not exist in the cloud yet. Most practices end up
combining trend-based and driver-based, which is what the Framework describes at its Run tier.

These are scored as a bucket because you can do them in any order. Plenty of teams inherit a cadence
from the finance calendar long before anyone writes down why the forecast exists. Others pick a
technique first and work out the horizon later.

[Action 39](/components/actions/039.md) scores how often the forecast actually gets refreshed, and its
guidance defines forward horizon, backwards horizon, frequency, and granularity. This action is about
what you decided and wrote down. A team that documents a monthly cadence but delivers quarterly earns
item 3 here and scores low on 39. Both numbers together tell you more than either one alone.

There is no percentage to calculate here. The approach is one document, so an item is met when you can
point to where the decision is written. If you have to infer it, it is not written.

Item 1 is the foundation, even though the order does not matter for scoring. A monthly,
driver-based, twelve-month forecast is either exactly right or badly over-built, and you cannot tell
which until someone writes down what decisions it feeds. The FinOps Framework frames forecasting as
telling you what you need to know to act now, rather than predicting the future.

## Scoring Guide

**Weight:** 1.0

### Formula

* Purpose recorded (the decisions the forecast exists to support, and the stakeholders who agreed them)
* Technique recorded (static, trend-based, or driver-based, stated for each organizational level the
  forecast is produced at)
* Cadence requirement recorded (when a forecast is due, and how often it is expected to be updated)
* Horizon recorded (how far forward the forecast reaches, how much history feeds it, and at what
  granularity)

10*Ceil(x/4)
