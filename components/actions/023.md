# 23: Configure Cost Spike Detection

**Specification Version:** 2.0.0

**Status:** Accepted

**Slug:** 23-configure-detect

**Implementation Types:**

* None

## Descriptive Statement

Configure detection for total cost spikes, cost/usage rate shifts, unit drops.

## Supplemental Guidance

The four items are detection configurations that stack rather than replace one another. A practice
running custom per team alerts still benefits from the provider's own alerts, because the two find
different things.

An alert on total spend catches large
obvious spikes. An alert per business unit catches the spike that is large for that team and invisible
in the total, and a unit cost alert catches the case where spend looks normal because usage grew
with it.

Coverage is not scored here. A practice can tick all four items and still have detection configured
for a fraction of its estate. Action 25 scores how much of the organization the resulting alerts
actually reach.

Every item here is something a practice configures. A person noticing that a number looks wrong is
not a configuration, and scoring it gives a practice with no detection at all a point for reading its
own bill. What happens once a spike surfaces belongs elsewhere: action 24 defines severity tiers and
response SLAs, and action 28 measures time to notify and time to validate. A spike is investigated
when it appears, not on a schedule, so nothing here asks for a review cadence.

## Scoring Guide

**Weight:** 1.0

### Formula

* Out-of-the-box tool based anomaly alerts (the anomaly detection shipped by the provider or cost tool
  is enabled)
* Custom simple increase alert calculations (the practice defines its own increase threshold, stating the
  comparison period and the percentage or amount that triggers it)
* Custom alerts per business unit, team, etc. (alert scopes are set per business unit, team or
  application rather than only on the total)
* Alerts per unit costs above thresholds (an alert is configured against a unit cost metric with a stated
  threshold)

10*Ceil(x/4)
