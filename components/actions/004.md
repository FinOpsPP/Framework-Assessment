# 4: Define Data Granularity & Frequency

**Specification Version:** 1.8.0

**Status:** Accepted

**Slug:** 4-define-granularity

**Implementation Types:**

* None

## Descriptive Statement

Define data granularity; include mandatory metadata.

## Supplemental Guidance

Scoring on this action is treated as sequential. If you have granularity
of say 2 days, for example, you have implicitly achieved all previous levels
of granularity.
Ideally, FinOps teams would have access to relevant spend and metric data instantly to make quick decisions. Getting real-time data has a cost, as does ingesting data at very granular levels. For example, to get data every second would require more processing and more storage costs to maintain. Thus, it may not always be necessary to have that level of granularity. When it comes to cost data, sometimes having granularity to the minute is useful when comparing with testing intervals of infrastructure. But after the analysis is complete, the data could be aggregated into hourly numbers instead.
On the other hand, sometimes the data is not granular enough. Perhaps combining cost data with customer onboarding metrics becomes difficult because the metrics are only at a daily granularity. That then limits the granularity of the unit economics you can calculate with the cost data.
When it comes to observability and business data, FinOps and supporting teams can decide on the granularity that would be needed. But sometimes that granularity is limited by what vendors are able to provide. Therefore, when scoring for this action, you can have a different score depending on the scope being evaluated. Or for a broad evaluation, focus on the primary spend data source and its granularity level.

## Scoring Guide

**Weight:** 1.0

### Formula

None

### Scoring Details

**Score Type:** threshold

| Score | Condition |
| ----- | --------- |
| 0 | Never |
| 1 | Monthly or greater |
| 2 | More than a week |
| 3 | 7 days |
| 4 | 5 days |
| 6 | 2 days |
| 8 | 1 day |
| 10 | Instant |
