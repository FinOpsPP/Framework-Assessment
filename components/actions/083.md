# 83: Allocate Upfront Costs Transparently

**Specification Version:** 2.0.0

**Status:** Accepted

**Slug:** 83-allocate-upfront

**Implementation Types:**

* None

## Descriptive Statement

Allocate upfront costs appropriately and transparently.

## Supplemental Guidance

These are scored as a bucket because you can do them in any order, though amortization usually comes
first because the other three are hard to explain to a team while a year of commitment lands in one
month's invoice.

No coverage rate applies. A commitment portfolio is a short list, so an assessor checks each one. A
percentage would round hard on a list of two.

This action is measured over every commitment, not only the ones with an upfront payment. Paying
nothing upfront is a legitimate way to preserve cash, and a practice that chooses it should not be
unable to score. Item 1 asks for cost reported across the term rather than in the month it is
invoiced, so an all-monthly portfolio satisfies it as soon as that reporting exists, because monthly
charges already fall in the months they cover. The item bites when a year of commitment lands in one
invoice and nobody spreads it.

Item 4 is the one teams skip. Reporting the unallocated amount is uncomfortable because it names the
cost nobody owns, and that is exactly why it belongs in the score. A practice that allocates 60% of
commitment cost and reports the other 40% is in better shape than one that allocates 60% and says
nothing.

The word "appropriately" in this action's description is not scored, because no two assessors would
read it the same way. What is scored is whether the rule is written down and whether the amount it
fails to place is reported.

This action covers the commitment slice only. Action 10 sets shared cost apportionment rules across the
whole practice and action 13 measures unallocated cost in general, both in the Allocation capability.
Item 4 here is deliberately narrower than action 13, counting only commitment cost.

## Scoring Guide

**Weight:** 1.0

### Formula

Measured over: every commitment held during the review period. These are few enough to enumerate, so
items are assessed against each commitment rather than as a coverage rate.

* Amortized view published (commitment cost is reported spread across the commitment term rather than
  landing in the month it is invoiced)
* Allocation rule documented (how the upfront amount and the remaining term charges reach the teams
  whose usage the commitment covers, including the treatment of unused commitment)
* Benefit visible to the team (a team can see the discounted rate it received, not only an aggregate
  saving for the organization)
* Unallocated commitment cost reported (the amount no team receives is reported in currency for the
  period)

10*Ceil(x/4)
