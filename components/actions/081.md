# 81: Purchase Commitments Regularly

**Specification Version:** 2.0.0

**Status:** Accepted

**Slug:** 81-purchase-commit

**Implementation Types:**

* None

## Descriptive Statement

Purchase commitments frequently; target healthy utilization.

## Supplemental Guidance

CRAWL (0-14) Purchases default to on-demand or to whatever was bought last time. Nobody computes a
savings rate, so nobody knows whether the last purchase returned anything.

WALK (15-30) Savings rate and coverage are both measured and purchases happen on a schedule, but the
numbers are reported after the fact rather than used to decide the next purchase.

RUN (31-42) The portfolio captures most of the savings its terms allow, coverage reaches the bulk of
eligible usage, purchases happen monthly or more often, and savings per dollar, break-even and
expected usage life are all recorded against each decision.

FLY (43-50) Purchases are triggered automatically by coverage and utilization reporting rather than
by a person watching a dashboard, and the portfolio is rebalanced against realized usage, so a
commitment that stops matching demand gets corrected rather than waited out.

Utilization is not scored on its own, and this is deliberate. Commitment waste is acceptable when the
savings justify it. A portfolio running at 70% utilization can beat one at 98% if it covers far more
usage and returns more money, and a practice chasing a high utilization number will get there by
buying less. Effective savings rate already nets the unused commitment out, which is why it carries
the heaviest weight.

Coverage is weighted second as the counterweight. A tiny, perfectly used portfolio can post a strong
savings rate while most of the bill still pays on-demand rates, and coverage is what exposes that.
The two numbers together say whether the portfolio is both well sized and well used.

The savings rate is scored against what the portfolio's terms allow, not against a flat percentage. A
one-year commitment yields far less discount than a three-year one, so a flat band would score a
practice on the length of its commitments rather than on how well it bought them. A team that buys
one-year terms because the usage genuinely ends in a year is doing the right thing and should not lose
points for it.

Scoring the raw rate also rewarded the wrong decision. Under a flat band, buying three-year terms on a
workload being retired would raise the score while stranding commitment nobody can use. Normalizing
removes that incentive.

Band 4 of the purchase criteria exists to catch a mistake the savings rate cannot show in time.
Buying a three-year term for a workload retiring in fifteen months scores well while the workload
still runs, because the commitment is fully used and the rate looks right. The damage lands in the
following period, once the usage ends and the commitment has nothing left to cover. Recording the
expected usage life and justifying the term against it is visible in the period the decision was
made, so it works as a leading indicator where the rate is a lagging one.

The expected-rate formula is practitioner judgment rather than a published figure. The Framework gives
a floor of more than 10% return on investment but states no expected rate by term, so the 25% base and
the 7.5% per additional year come from observed commitment discounts. The coverage bands are ours for
the same reason. Treat both as a starting point for discussion.

The word "healthy" in this action's description is not scored, because no two assessors would read it
the same way. Utilization still belongs in the conversation, and action 82 scores whether anyone is
told when it declines.

Action 79 scores the documented commitment strategy and its guardrails. Action 80 scores building the
repeatable coverage, utilization, and breakeven analysis. This action scores the purchasing that follows
from them, so a practice can hold a good strategy on paper and still score poorly here.

## Scoring Guide

**Weight:** 1.0

### Formula

* Weighted sum
  Effective savings rate (score x4),
  Commitment coverage (score x3),
  Purchase cadence (score x2),
  Purchase criteria (score x1).
  Max raw (50)

* Effective savings rate achieved on the portfolio, net of unused commitment, scored as a share of
  the rate the portfolio's own commitment terms make available
  (expected rate = 25% + 7.5% x (weighted-average commitment term in years - 1), so one-year terms
  expect 25%, two-year 32.5%, and three-year 40%)
  (0=Not measured, 1<50%, 2=50-69%, 3=70-84%, 4=85-99%, 5=100% or higher)
* Share of eligible usage covered by a commitment over the review period
  (0=Not measured, 1<25%, 2=25-49%, 3=50-69%, 4=70-84%, 5=85% or higher)
* How often commitments are purchased rather than left to renew in bulk
  (0=None purchased, 1=One-off, 2=Annual, 3=Quarterly, 4=Monthly or more often,
  5=Purchases triggered automatically by coverage and utilization reporting)
* Which numeric tests a purchase must pass before approval
  (0=None, 1=A savings target stated informally, 2=Savings per dollar of commitment recorded,
  3=Break-even window also recorded, 4=Expected usage life recorded and the term justified against it,
  5=All three applied to each decision and the portfolio rebalanced against realized usage)

* Output Range (0-50)

and the break-even window in the purchase criteria are the tolerance statements.

spread over the year rather than bought in bulk.
